The Problem

International students navigate housing blind

Every year, thousands of international students arrive in new cities with little local knowledge, limited personal networks, and no reliable way to verify the safety of a neighborhood or landlord. They rely on word-of-mouth, biased reviews, and platforms built for general audiences — not for their specific vulnerabilities.

The consequences go beyond inconvenience. The gap between needing housing and finding housing that is safe, inclusive, and trustworthy is a genuine wellbeing crisis.

User Research

Safety isn't a feature — it's the foundation

Research was conducted through interviews and surveys with international students currently enrolled in U.S. universities. The findings revealed that housing search is not just a logistical challenge — it's an emotionally charged process driven by fear of the unknown.

100%
of participants prioritized safety as their top concern when searching for housing — above price, commute, or amenities.
75%
relied on personal networks — friends from home or current students — as their primary source of housing leads, because they couldn't trust platforms alone.
50%
found the process "extremely challenging" and described feeling overwhelmed, unprepared, or unsafe during their housing search.

Key insight: Students don't just want more listings. They want a way to trust what they're seeing — verified information, reviews from people like them, and clarity about what a neighborhood is actually like before committing.

Design Solution

Four features built around what students actually need

Each feature maps directly back to a research finding — no design for design's sake. The goal was to reduce anxiety at every step of the housing search, from discovery through move-in.

🗺️
Verified Map Search
Browse listings on a safety-aware map with neighborhood data, crime overlays, and proximity to campus — all in one view.
⭐
Student-Only Reviews
Reviews written by verified students — not anonymous web users. Filter by identity or student background for more relevant signal.
🔍
Advanced Filters
Filter by budget, proximity, pet policy, LGBTQ+-friendly status, accessibility features, and more — the details that matter most get surfaced, not buried.
💬
University Community Inbox
A dedicated inbox connecting students with verified university housing offices and landlords — keeping official communication in one trusted place.
Design Process

From lo-fi to hi-fi — clarity at every stage

The design process followed a standard but rigorous path: IA → paper sketches → lo-fi wireframes → usability testing → hi-fi screens. The lo-fi wireframes were particularly valuable for testing the map interaction and filter flow before committing to visual design.

Reflection

Designing with, not for

This project reinforced something I carry into every engagement: the more marginalized or vulnerable the user, the higher the stakes for getting design right. International students aren't a niche audience — they're a population navigating one of the most uncertain transitions of their lives.

The most important design decision wasn't a feature — it was the decision to center safety and identity explicitly, rather than treating them as edge cases. That choice shaped everything else: the filters, the review system, the community inbox. Good research makes those choices obvious.
