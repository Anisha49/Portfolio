The Challenge

Personal styling is broken for most people

Professional personal styling is expensive, time-consuming, and out of reach for most everyday users. Meanwhile, existing wardrobe apps fail to learn and adapt — leaving users with generic suggestions that don't reflect their actual wardrobe, schedule, or taste.

🧩
Lack of Styling Knowledge
Users don't know how to style their existing pieces together for specific occasions and seasons.
⏱️
Time Constraints
High-profile professionals lack the time to shop or curate their wardrobe thoughtfully.
🔄
Service Limitations
Existing subscription services fail to adapt to specific user tastes over time.
💸
Accessibility Gap
Personal styling is expensive and inaccessible to everyday users without a large budget.
The Solution

An AI stylist that learns you

StyleIO is an integrated mobile and desktop application that leverages generative AI to act as a personal stylist — making professional styling accessible, adaptive, and convenient for everyone.

01
Wardrobe Integration
Upload inspiration photos from Pinterest or your camera roll to receive recommendations based on your existing wardrobe — not what you need to buy.
02
Personalized Recommendations
AI suggests outfits tailored to occasions, seasons, and your unique style goals — with a feedback loop that gets smarter over time.
03
Integrated Shopping
Missing a key piece? The app surfaces new items from nearby stores that complement what you already own.
04
Smart Scheduling
A calendar feature proactively suggests outfits for upcoming events — networking sessions, interviews, casual weekends.

Usability Testing Findings

Three problems the first design didn't solve

Mid-fidelity prototypes were tested with participants before moving to high-fidelity. Three consistent issues surfaced across sessions and directly shaped the final design.

🗺️
Navigation Issues
Users found the mobile information architecture confusing, and icons were difficult to interpret compared to the more spacious desktop version.
💬
Lack of Feedback
The initial design lacked clear confirmation screens for actions like selecting items or completing purchases — leaving users unsure if their action registered.
🔍
Missing Context
Participants wanted more interactive screens where the AI gathers additional details upfront — occasion, weather, formality — to better tailor suggestions from the start.

Key Interactions

Where the AI gets smarter

Three interaction patterns were critical to making the AI feel genuinely useful — not just decorative.

👍
Like / Dislike Feedback
Simple feedback mechanism on every suggestion. When users dislike an outfit, the AI asks a follow-up question — "Too casual? Wrong color?" — to refine future results with precision.
💬
Contextual AI Prompts
Before generating suggestions, the AI asks targeted follow-up questions — occasion, weather, how formal — so recommendations land accurately on the first try.
🔎
Advanced Filters (Desktop)
The desktop interface includes precise search filtering by size, category, and occasion — surfacing exactly the right items from the wardrobe and integrated shopping catalog.
Conclusion

What this project taught me

Consistency Across Platforms
Building a unified design system upfront saved enormous time when translating mobile flows to desktop — and vice versa.
Testing Changes Everything
Mid-fi usability testing transformed a confusing navigation into a streamlined experience — without any extra features.
AI Needs to Earn Trust
The feedback loop (like/dislike + follow-up) was the most critical design decision — it transformed the AI from "generic" to genuinely personal.
