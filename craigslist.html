Overview

The problem with Craigslist

Craigslist is one of the most widely used online marketplaces in the world — covering goods, services, housing, and jobs. But despite its reach, the site is widely perceived as chaotic and poorly organized. Finding what you need requires knowing the site well, and first-time or infrequent users frequently get lost.

Our team was tasked with evaluating and restructuring Craigslist's Information Architecture to create a more intuitive experience — without breaking what its loyal users already know.

"The current site has too many categories, inconsistent naming, and no clear hierarchy. A user looking for a nanny, a car, and an apartment shouldn't have to navigate three completely different systems."

Research Process

Three methods, one clear picture

We used a layered research approach to understand both what content exists and how users expect it to be organized — then validated our restructured IA with real task-based testing.

Content Inventory
Card Sorting
Treejack Testing

Content Inventory — We catalogued every main category and UI element on the current Craigslist site, identifying primary friction points in navigation pathways, inconsistent category naming conventions, and search functionality limitations.

Card Sorting — We engaged users to group content items as they made sense to them, revealing the mental models that were dramatically different from Craigslist's current structure — particularly around Services, Community, and For Sale categories.

Treejack Testing — With the restructured IA in hand, we tested 10 participants on three representative tasks to validate the new structure and identify remaining gaps.

Treejack Results

Task-by-task validation

Task 1 — Browse for a vehicle
90%
Clear category placement; users navigated with minimal friction
Task 2 — Search for pet services
80%

50%

Action taken: The 50% posting success rate directly informed a new dedicated category to clarify navigation and improve the posting experience — one of the most impactful design decisions in the project.

New Information Architecture

7 clear categories, built from user mental models

The final IA was refined into seven primary top-level categories — each based directly on what card sorting revealed about how users naturally group content.

For Sale / For Rent
Separated into distinct sections — non-rent items no longer bleed into the Rent section.
Services
Tailored specifically to assistance tasks: health, gardening, travel, and care.
Jobs
Grouped by niche (e.g., Science & Technology) with sub-pages — no more overwhelming flat lists.
Discussion / Community
Streamlined to remove redundant categories and focus on engagement and local connections.
Utility Nav
Centralized Search, Login, Account, Support, and "Post an Ad" for easy, persistent access.
Housing
Dedicated section with clear sub-categories for apartments, rooms, sublets, and commercial.
Wireframe Decisions

Simplicity as the design principle

Every wireframe decision was grounded in reducing cognitive load. We introduced a minimalist, modern interface that preserves Craigslist's utility while making it feel accessible to new users.

Icon-based Categories
Clear visual hierarchy on the homepage — icons make categories scannable at a glance.
Enhanced Filtering
Visible, persistent filter, view, and sort buttons — no hunting for controls.
Integrated Messaging
In-app chatbox to contact sellers directly, removing the friction of external email threads.
Simplified Posting
Streamlined posting flow using location selection and QR code image uploads.
